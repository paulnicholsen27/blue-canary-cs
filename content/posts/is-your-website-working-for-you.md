You use the Internet so you know this is true: If a website takes too long to load, most people will leave before they even see it.

The truth is that the internet has trained all of us to be impatient. In fact, studies show that after only *six seconds* you've lost 30% of your users. After only four more seconds, you've lost another 40%!

We’ve all done it. Click a link, wait a few seconds, then back out and try something else.

Now imagine that happening to your business every day.

## **First Impressions Happen Fast**

When someone lands on your site, they’re making snap decisions:

Does this look trustworthy? Can I find what I need quickly? *Is this business professional*?

If the answer to any of those is “not really,” they’re gone.

It doesn't matter if you might be the perfect business for them and able to give exactly what they're looking for, but your website is quietly turning new people away before they ever contact you.

## **Speed Matters More Than You Think**

Website speed isn’t just a technical detail. It’s directly tied to:

How many people stay on your site

How high you show up in Google

How many leads or inquiries you get

A slow site doesn’t just feel annoying. It signals that something might be off.  If your website is unprofessional, customers will start wondering what else you're not doing right at your business.

On the flip side, a fast site feels smooth, modern, and trustworthy.  And potential customers notice that.

**Mobile Isn’t Optional Anymore**

For better or worse, most people are visiting your site on their phone.

If your site is hard to read, or the formatting is messed up, or the buttons don't work on mobile, people won't stick around trying to make it work.  A good mobile experience isn’t about shrinking your desktop site. It requires a mobile-first design approach to ensure that your site is perfect no matter what size screen it's loaded on.

## **Clarity Beats Cleverness**

A lot of websites try to be clever with overblown design and fancy animations that tend to annoy more than they inspire.  Visitors aren’t looking to be impressed with technology they don't understand in the first place. They want to know who you are and how you can help them.

If someone has to think too hard to answer those questions, you’ve already lost them.

*Clear always wins.*

## **Small Changes Can Make a Big Difference**

The good news is that fixing a website doesn’t always mean starting from scratch.

Sometimes it’s cleaning up the layout and making navigation simpler.  It could be changing what's happening under the hood to speed up the site. Or strengthening calls-to-action so your customer is encouraged to make a move.

Other times, a full redesign makes more sense.  If your site looks like it was created in the 90s by a middle schooler designing their MySpace page, you're going to want to bring it up to date.

Either way, the goal is the same: make it easy for the right people to find you, trust you, and reach out.

## **Your Website Should Work For You**

Your website isn’t just an online brochure. It’s a tool that is focused on increasing the quality of your business.

A good website needs to bring in new customers while supporting your existing ones in an easy, straightforward, professional way.  If it’s not doing those things, it’s worth taking a closer look.
