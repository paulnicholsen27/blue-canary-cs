Your website doesn't complain. It doesn't send you a memo saying "hey, three people left today because they couldn't find your phone number." It just sits there, looking fine to you, while a chunk of your potential customers click away and go call your competitor instead.

If you built your site a few years ago, or a well-meaning nephew put it together over a weekend, there's a decent chance it's doing exactly this. Here are the signs to watch for, and what's actually worth fixing first.

## 1. It takes more than a few seconds to load

People do not wait around anymore. If your site is still loading while someone's finger is already hovering over the back button, you've lost them before they've even seen what you offer. Slow loading is often caused by oversized images straight from a phone camera, too many plugins, or hosting that was fine in 2015 and hasn't been touched since.

The fix is usually cheaper than people expect: compressing images, cleaning up unused plugins, and switching to decent hosting can solve most of this without a full rebuild.

## 2. It looks fine on your laptop and terrible on a phone

Here's a number that tends to surprise business owners: more than half of website visits now come from a phone, not a desktop or laptop. If your site was designed years ago and never properly adapted, chances are it's cramped, the buttons are too small to tap, and the text runs off the edge of the screen on mobile.

Pull up your own site on your phone right now, genuinely, and try to book an appointment or find your hours the way a stranger would. If it's a struggle for you, it's a bigger struggle for someone who's never seen your business before and has three other tabs open.

## 3. Nobody can tell what you actually do in the first five seconds

Visitors decide almost instantly whether they've landed in the right place. If your homepage opens with a vague slogan and a stock photo of people laughing at a laptop, instead of a clear line like "Plumbing repairs in Newtown, same-day service," you're making people work to figure out if you're relevant to them. Most won't bother.

## 4. Your contact information is playing hide and seek

This one sounds almost too simple to matter, and yet it's one of the most common problems out there. Phone number buried in a footer nobody scrolls to. No address. A contact form that quietly fails to send. If someone has to hunt for how to reach you, plenty will just give up and pick the business one search result down that made it obvious.

Your phone number and a clear way to contact you should be visible on every page, not just tucked away on a "Contact Us" page three clicks deep.

## 5. It hasn't been updated since a while ago and it shows

An outdated copyright year in the footer, a "current promotions" page advertising last year's holiday sale, staff photos of people who no longer work there. None of this is fatal on its own, but altogether it sends a quiet signal: this business might not be paying close attention right now. And if a website looks neglected, people tend to assume the business behind it might be too.

## 6. There's no way to tell it's really you

Small business owners win on trust, and trust online comes from specifics: real photos of your actual work or your actual shop, real reviews, a real name and face behind the business. A site built entirely from generic stock photography and vague testimonials ("Great service! - J.") doesn't give a stranger much reason to pick you over the next search result.

## 7. It doesn't show up when people search for what you do

You can have the nicest looking site in your town and still lose every customer who never finds it. If you're not showing up when someone searches "your service + your town," the site isn't doing its job no matter how good it looks. This usually comes down to a few fixable things: a claimed and filled-out Google Business Profile, your city and service actually mentioned in your page text (not just implied), and basic technical health like the loading speed and mobile issues mentioned above.
